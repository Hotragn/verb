The question this answers: how many decisions can your team actually check before checking turns into signing?

AI agents can now produce more decisions in a week than your people can review in a month. When that happens, the extra decisions do not stop. They get approved anyway, by somebody with eleven minutes and nineteen items in a queue. The approval is recorded. The checking is not.

Nobody chooses to rubber-stamp. It is simply what running out of review capacity looks like from the inside, and there is no error message for it. This page puts a number on the capacity you have, so you can compare it against the work you are sending.

Fill in four numbers per row and read the answer. Nothing is sent anywhere: this page has no network requests, no analytics and no sign-up. It works with your laptop offline.

What the four numbers mean, in plain terms
R
How many people are genuinely qualified to judge this kind of decision. Not everyone with the approve button. The people whose sign-off you would defend if something went wrong.
H
Hours per week each of those people has nominally set aside for reviewing.
u
The share of those hours that survives meetings, holidays and everything else. Between 0 and 1. If you have not measured it, 0.5 is a fair starting guess. Above 0.7 is almost always wishful.
c
Hours it takes one qualified person to genuinely check one decision. This is the number that matters most, and the one nobody has. Measure it: time thirty reviews, then ask each reviewer afterwards whether they really checked, and throw away the ones who say no.
D
How many decisions of this kind get produced per week, by agents and people together.
k
Optional. If a second agent checks the first agent's work and closes some of it without a human, this is the share it closes. Only count it if you have measured how often that checker misses a bad decision. If you have not, leave it at zero.

What to do with this. If any row comes back as an overdraft, that class is the one to act on, and the table above it tells you which of the four levers could close the gap. Two of them have real range: making the check cheaper, and sending less work for review. Hiring is usually neither fast enough nor available.

The full specification, the decision classifier, the drift detector and the eval gates are in the repository. A PMO director can implement the whole model from the README with no code at all.

Estimated inputs give you an estimated answer. The formula turns your assumptions into arithmetic; it does not turn them into evidence. Measure c before you present the result, and say out loud that reviewers work faster when observed, which makes the budget look better than it is.
