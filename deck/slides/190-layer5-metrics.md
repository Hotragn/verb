## Six numbers. Always per class, never averaged.

| | what it tells you | target |
|---|---|---|
| **budget** | how much review you actually have | measure it, do not target it |
| **overdraft** | whether you are spending capacity you have | at or under 1.0 |
| **silent drift** | whether the approvals are real | near baseline |
| **containment** | how much review the agents genuinely supply | measured, with its miss rate |
| **escalation precision** | whether escalations are worth the time they cost | 0.7 or better |
| **reversal latency** | whether the undo button is real | inside one review cycle |

<p class="small">Average these across a portfolio and a healthy Class A will hide
a drowning Class C. In the worked example the portfolio number is 0.2 and looks
excellent.</p>

@notes

Do not read the table. Point at row three and say that is the one that matters,
and the next slide is entirely about it.

The averaging warning is worth ten seconds because it is the mistake everyone
makes on their first dashboard. One healthy class covers a failing one and the
board sees a green light.

If somebody asks why only six: because a PMO director has to be able to hold
them, and because every one of them changes a decision. A metric that does not
change a decision is a chart.
