<p class="small"><strong>Put drift on the same page as the good news.</strong>
On its own page nobody looks at it, and the four green numbers win.</p>

@notes

This is the most useful slide in Part 2 and it is worth slowing down for.

Every one of those four numbers is a metric a real PMO reports today. Not one of
them can see the failure. Worse, three of them move in the direction that looks
like success while the failure gets worse: approvals rise because approving got
faster, cycle time falls for the same reason, and reversals fall because nobody
is looking hard enough to find the errors.

The reversal rate is the cruellest one. It falls, and it looks like quality
improving. It is falling because detection stopped.

Then the instruction, which is a governance decision rather than a technical one:
put drift on the same page as the four green numbers. Not in an appendix, not on
a separate dashboard. On the same page, where a board has to look at it in the
same glance.

The figures here are illustrative. Say so.
