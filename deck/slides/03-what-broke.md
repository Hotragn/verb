## The number that did not move is review.

<div class="cols">
<div>
<h4>Went up, a lot</h4>
<ul>
<li>Decisions produced per week</li>
<li>Speed of production</li>
<li>How polished the output looks</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div>
<h4>Did not move</h4>
<ul>
<li>People qualified to judge the decision</li>
<li>Hours they actually have</li>
<li>Time one honest check takes</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>

<p class="lead">When production runs past review capacity, the extra work does not
stop. It gets approved anyway.</p>

@notes

The right-hand column is the point. None of those three improved when the models
improved, and two of them got worse.

Worse how: the artifacts now arrive fluent. A confident, well-written, wrong
document is harder to check than a rough one, because there is nothing to snag
on.

If the room is senior, this is where you will see the first nod of genuine
recognition. Wait for it before moving on.
