## Four things you can do on Monday.

<ol>
<li><strong>Pick one decision type</strong> an agent already touches. One. Not a
programme.</li>
<li><strong>Time thirty reviews of it.</strong> Then ask each reviewer whether they
genuinely checked, and throw away the ones who say no. That last step is the
whole thing.</li>
<li><strong>Do the division.</strong> Four numbers into the calculator. It takes
about a minute and it runs in your browser with nothing sent anywhere.</li>
<li><strong>Show somebody the gap.</strong> Not a programme, not a policy. One
number, one conversation.</li>
</ol>

<p class="small">If the gap is under 1.0 you have headroom and can deploy more.
If it is over, you now know something you did not know this morning, and that is
the entire first stage.</p>

@notes

This is what they leave with, so make it feel small. Everything before this was
the argument; this is the ask, and the ask has to be doable by one person without
permission.

Step two is the one that gets skipped and it is the one that makes the number
mean anything. Without that question you are measuring how long approvals take,
not how long checking takes, and under overdraft those are different quantities.

End on the last line rather than on an exhortation. Knowing the gap is genuinely
the whole of stage one, and saying so lowers the barrier.
