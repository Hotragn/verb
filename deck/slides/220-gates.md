## Four gates, each with a number attached.

<div class="cols">
<div>
<h4>Before it runs</h4>
<ul>
<li><strong>Do we agree what class this is?</strong> Two qualified people classify
fifty decisions separately. If they disagree, you do not know what checking costs.</li>
<li><strong>Would it have been right on history?</strong> Replay it against
decisions you already know the outcome of. Zero Class D taken on its own.</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div>
<h4>While it runs</h4>
<ul>
<li><strong>Is every decision checkable at the cost we assumed?</strong> Continuous,
plus twenty read by a human for substance.</li>
<li><strong>Is the containment real?</strong> Quarterly, against decisions known to
be bad.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>

<p class="small">A gate without a number attached to it is a meeting.</p>

@notes

That last line usually gets a laugh. Let it, then make the serious point: every
one of these has a threshold in the repo, and the runners are there too, so this
is not a slide of good intentions.

Gate one fails more often than teams expect, and that failure is the most useful
thing it produces. If two qualified people cannot agree what class something is,
every number downstream is decoration.

The replay gate has a criterion people find strange and it is worth the thirty
seconds: if the agent disagrees with history and turns out to be right, it still
fails unless a reviewer could adjudicate that disagreement inside the budgeted
time. A right answer nobody can check is not usable.
