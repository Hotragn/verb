## What I do not know.

<ul>
<li><strong>The measurement is biased.</strong> People check more carefully when
watched, so the budget always looks better than it is. Direction known, size not.</li>
<li><strong>Bursts are not modelled.</strong> The budget is a rate. Work does not
arrive evenly. You can be fine on average and still drop reviews in board week.
This is the biggest hole and I am asking for help with it.</li>
<li><strong>Timing is a proxy for checking.</strong> Somebody who already knows the
answer can approve correctly in twenty seconds.</li>
<li><strong>The B and C line is genuinely blurry,</strong> and nobody has published
how often two people disagree.</li>
<li><strong>There is no field data.</strong> Every figure in this talk is
illustrative. The repository says so on the same page it prints them.</li>
</ul>

@notes

Do not apologise your way through this slide. Deliver it flat and fast. It buys
more credibility than anything else in the talk, and a framework that hides its
weak points gets adopted and then quietly dropped.

The bursts one is a real invitation. If anybody in the room knows queueing theory
you want them to find you afterwards, so say that out loud.

On the last point: be blunt that the numbers are illustrative. Somebody will
screenshot a figure and repost it, and you want to have said this clearly enough
that it is on record.
