## Deploy where checking is cheap, not where the task is easy.

<div class="quad">
  <div class="go"><div class="qt">deploy</div>
    <div class="qd">Model is good at it and checking is cheap. This is the whole
    game and it is less glamorous than the other box.</div></div>
  <div class="hot"><div class="qt">the trap</div>
    <div class="qd">Model is good at it and checking is expensive. Every
    impressive demo lives here. So does every overdraft.</div></div>
  <div><div class="qt">wait</div>
    <div class="qd">Cheap to check, model is not there yet. Harmless. Revisit
    next year.</div></div>
  <div><div class="qt">do not</div>
    <div class="qd">Expensive to check and the model is weak. No path from here.</div></div>
</div>

<div class="margin"><b>Horizontal</b><br>cost of checking, cheap on the left.<br><br>
<b>Vertical</b><br>how good the model is, better at the top.</div>

@notes

Point at the top right and name three things that live there. Portfolio
prioritisation. Vendor dispute strategy. Strategic dependency renegotiation.

Say the awkward part: the model is genuinely good at all three. That is not the
problem. The problem is that your review capacity for them is a couple of dozen a
week, so the deployment is capped at a couple of dozen a week no matter how good
the model gets.

You do not fix the top right by deploying harder. You fix it by moving work to
the left, which means making the decision cheaper to check. That is engineering
work, not a renaming exercise.
