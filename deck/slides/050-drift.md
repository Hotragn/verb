## Nobody decides to rubber-stamp.

<p class="lead">A reviewer with eleven minutes and nineteen items in the queue
produces nineteen approvals.</p>

<p>Every one is recorded as a review. The governance record is intact. The
governance is not. There is no error message for this, and it looks exactly like
a working PMO right up until something goes wrong and nobody can reconstruct who
checked what.</p>

<hr>

<p class="small"><strong>Silent drift.</strong> The gap between approvals recorded
and reviews genuinely done. Silent because the system records it as success.</p>

@notes

Slow right down here. This is the emotional centre of the talk and it needs
space.

Say plainly that this is not a discipline problem and you are not blaming
reviewers. The reviewer is behaving rationally under a constraint somebody else
handed them. If you get this wrong the room gets defensive and you lose them.

The line to land: rubber-stamping is not a behaviour, it is what running out of
review capacity looks like from the inside. Then pause and let it sit.

If you want a story here, use one from your own experience rather than a
hypothetical. It has to be real to work.
