## Five stages, and the test for which one you are on.

```small
   S0            S1            S2            S3            S4
   unmeasured    measured      bounded       contained     self-budgeting
   |             |             |             |             |
   you do not    you know      you say no    agents supply the system
   know          the gap       to work       verified      throttles
                                             capacity      itself
   |             |             |             |             |
   +-------------+-------------+-------------+-------------+----X  no S5
```

<p class="lead">The test is one question. What does it cost you to check one
expert decision, and when did you last measure it?</p>

<p class="small">If there is no number and no date, you are at S0. Most
organisations that describe themselves as S2 are at S0 with better paperwork.</p>

@notes

Do not walk all five. Give them the one-question test and let them place
themselves privately. That lands harder than a description.

The hard step is S1 to S2, and it is not technical. It is being willing to say no
to a deployment that is over budget. Everything before that is measurement and
everything after is engineering.

Then why there is no S5, which is the part people remember. S5 would be a system
that sets its own risk appetite and audits its own drift: no human in the loop at
all. That is not more mature, it is the removal of the thing the model exists to
manage.

The line: such a system might still be right. It just could not be known to be
right by the people running it, and an organisation that cannot know is not
governing, it is hoping.
